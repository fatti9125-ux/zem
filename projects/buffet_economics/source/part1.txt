From a triathlete who got banned for eating a hundred plates of sushi, to forty people brawling inside a Golden Corral over steak, this is how all-you-can-eat buffets make money when people eat like this.

At a lot of Golden Corrals in America right now, about seventeen dollars gets you dinner. Not a dinner. All of it. Carved sirloin, fried chicken, shrimp, mac and cheese, a dessert bar, and roughly a hundred and fifty other things, for as long as you can keep walking back.

Nobody counts your plates. Nobody taps you on the shoulder on trip number five.

That makes the next part strange. For years, the biggest name in American buffets wasn't Golden Corral. It was Old Country Buffet, with more than 350 restaurants at its peak. Its parent company went bankrupt in 2008, then again in 2012, then again in 2016. By 2021, there wasn't a single Old Country Buffet left, and most of the furniture had been sold at auction.

So the obvious answer, that buffets make money because most people don't eat that much, can't be the whole story. Both chains had the same kind of customers, the same unlimited plates and, I'm fairly confident, the same mashed potatoes. One is still opening new restaurants. The other one is a memory.

To work out why, we need a number buffets don't put on the sign: what it actually costs them to feed you.

According to the Wall Street Journal, about 38 cents of every dollar Golden Corral takes in goes on food. On a seventeen-dollar dinner, that's roughly six dollars and fifty cents of food per person.

Six-fifty. That's the budget, for a meal where the customer is allowed to try everything twice.

Of course, nobody eats an average meal. When The Hustle modeled a typical twenty-dollar buffet, it split customers into three types. The average eater cost the restaurant about $7.40 in food. Someone who mostly went for bread and potatoes cost about $4.70. And the person who walked past the salad and went straight back for more meat, let's call him Gary, cost about $16.90.

In that model, only about one diner in twenty eats like Gary. And the restaurant doesn't need to make money on Gary. It needs the other nineteen people to quietly cover him.
